## Story plan
- **Thesis:** Unspent gift card money is not grabbed when a card goes quiet: companies predict from their own history how much will never be spent and book that share as sales, a little at a time.
- **Central question:** Who keeps the money on an unspent gift card?
- **Conflict:** wants The store wants revenue it can count on; the holder wants their balance.; obstacle Cards that never expire can't simply be taken back.; stakes Starbucks booked $222.4 million this way in one year.
- **Misconception:** The store takes the money when a card expires.
- **Original angle:** The company's own filing explains the forecast in one line; many explainers stop at 'stores profit from forgotten cards'.
- **Payoff:** The store keeps the predicted share, a little at a time; part can go to the government; the rest is still yours to spend.

## Final script


**[Hook]**

In the US, Starbucks cards don't expire. Yet by our math, Starbucks booked $222.4 million of card money as sales in fiscal 2025, with no drink served. _(b1, i1)_


**[The forecast]**

The reason is a forecast. From its own history, Starbucks estimates how much will never be redeemed, and calls that share breakage. _(b2, c2)_


**[In step with others]**

None of this touches your balance. Each time a card gets spent, a predicted slice moves into sales. _(b3, c2)_


**[The state]**

Part can also go to the government under unclaimed property laws. _(b4, c2)_


**[Who keeps it]**

So who keeps the money? The store keeps the money it predicted you'd never spend, but only if you never do. Your balance is still yours. Spend it, and prove the forecast wrong. _(b5, c2)_
