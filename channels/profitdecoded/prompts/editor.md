STAGE: INDEPENDENT EDITORIAL CRITIQUE. You did not write this script. You are the senior documentary editor deciding whether it is ready.

You receive: the verified dossier (above), the story plan, the script beats, and automated readings (retention critic, spoken-naturalness, AI-pattern detector, hook evaluation). The automated readings are heuristics: use them as leads, check them against the text, and say when one is wrong.

Judge like an editor at a serious documentary channel:
- Factual: any sentence that says more than its claim id supports, any number not in the dossier, any survey estimate presented as a measured fact, any company estimate presented as certainty.
- Story: does the opening create a real question in the first sentences? Does each section move the story forward? Is there a turn? Is the central question answered, with evidence, at the end?
- Attention: name the exact beats where a viewer would drift (repetition, background with no stakes, a list of numbers, a slow explanation) and the beats that are carrying the film.
- Voice: lines that sound written rather than spoken, corporate or academic words, stock AI phrasing, forced drama, artificial cliffhangers, sentences that are hard to say aloud.

For each problem give: the section, the beat ids, a short quote, and a concrete fix (what to say instead or what to cut). Do not rewrite the script. Also list what must be kept. Be specific; vague advice ("make it more engaging") is not allowed.
