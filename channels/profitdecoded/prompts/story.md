STAGE: STORY PLAN. Do not write narration yet. Decide what this documentary argues and how it unfolds.

Work only from the verified claims in the dossier above. If a claim is not there, it does not exist for this film.

Decide, in this order:
1. Thesis: one sentence that argues something a viewer could disagree with before watching. Not a topic ("how gift cards make money") but a claim ("unused balances are not a loss for the store; they are revenue it schedules in advance").
2. Central question: the single question the film opens and must answer by the end.
3. Conflict: who wants what, what stands in the way, and what is at stake for the viewer. Real tension from the evidence, never invented villains.
4. Misconception: what most viewers believe at the start that the evidence will correct.
5. Original angle: what this film shows that the listed competitor coverage does not. Be concrete.
6. Hooks: at least 6 opening lines built on different mechanisms (contradiction, number, hidden incentive, consumer pain, visual mystery, financial paradox, question). Each must be literally true per the dossier: no number, absolute or superlative the dossier does not support.
7. Sections: an outline where every section has one job (setup, question, evidence, mechanism, complication, turn, consequence, caveat, payoff), the claim ids it uses, and the questions it raises or resolves. Each section must add something the previous ones did not. Put the strongest surprise where attention would otherwise dip, not at a fixed interval. Every question raised must be resolved later. Include at least one complication or turn in the middle and one honest caveat.
8. Payoff: the answer to the central question, stated plainly, plus the implication for the viewer.
9. Evidence: thesisClaimIds and payoffClaimIds list the claims behind them; the payoff uses only claims a section establishes. Conclude nothing the claims do not state (no "most", no "exact split"); if the evidence cannot answer part of the question, say so.
10. One home per figure: a section's claimIds are the facts it establishes, each figure-bearing claim once. Hook, caveat and payoff may refer back; no section restates its numbers.

Avoid: fake suspense, teasers that delay information ("more on that later"), structures that repeat the same point with new words, a conclusion that summarises the intro.
