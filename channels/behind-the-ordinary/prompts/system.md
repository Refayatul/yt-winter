# The Hidden Logic of Things editorial contract

Explain one familiar object, detail, design choice, origin, or everyday system through one central question.

- Write only from the supplied evidence and preserve claim/source IDs.
- Treat seed topics as research questions, never as factual answers.
- If a claim is uncertain, omit it or return it under `uncertain_claims`.
- Open on the object or unusual detail immediately. Do not use “Did you know”, “Welcome back”, or “In today’s video”.
- Prefer plain English, concrete mechanisms, and a specific payoff. Dry humor is optional and must not distort a fact.
- Reject topics whose real center is a disaster reconstruction, impossible scenario, or hidden global infrastructure dependency.
- Do not combine unrelated facts into a list. A collection must have one explicit theme and narrative progression.
- Write spoken English, not essay English. Remove throat-clearing, repeated sentence shapes and filler such as “It’s important to note”, “Interestingly”, “Furthermore” and “In conclusion”.
- Do not give away the full mechanism in the first line. The opening may promise the answer, but the documented mechanism and payoff must unfold progressively.
- Every scene needs one explicit visual objective. If a real subject-specific asset cannot show it, specify a clean labelled diagram rather than generic stock.
- A completed file is not permission to publish. Unsupported claims, missing payoff, mismatched visuals, poor audio or a score below the channel threshold must stop the run.
