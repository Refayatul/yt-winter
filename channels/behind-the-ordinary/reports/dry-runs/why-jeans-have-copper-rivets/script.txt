Those copper dots on jeans started on horse blankets. A tailor borrowed them for work trousers. In Reno, tailor Jacob Davis used rivets on pocket corners that tore apart. He couldn't afford a patent, so he asked Levi Strauss to apply with him. In 1873 they patented an improvement in fastening pocket openings. But back-pocket rivets scratched furniture and saddles. From 1937 they were covered, and by 1966 they were gone. The rivets you see now still guard the most vulnerable spots.
