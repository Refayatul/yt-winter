On May 18, 1980, the bulging north flank of Mount St. Helens collapsed in the largest landslide ever recorded, releasing a sideways blast.

Failure chain: Magma intrudes, north flank bulges → Bulge grows ~1.5 m per day → M5.1 quake triggers landslide → Unroofed magma blasts sideways → Forest flattened, lahars downstream
